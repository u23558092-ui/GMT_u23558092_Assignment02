Across the 218 World Bank-classified economies for FY27, the dashboard shows the disparities in income across different regions as well as clustering within regions.
High-income economies represent the largest single tier globally (39.9%), followed by upper-middle-income (27.1%), lower-middle-income (21.6%) and lastly low-income economies (11.5%). 
Wealth can be seen to be heavily concentrated in Europe and Central Asia, as well as North America.
Compared to Sub-Saharan Africa, it is shown that this region accounts for 84.0% of all low-income economies worldwide, with 41 of its 48 economies falling into either the low- or lower-middle-income brackets. 
Further, high-income economies are largely unclassified for World Bank borrowing, but low-income economies can be found to fall under International Development Association (IDA) financing or Blend facilities.
Lastly, middle income economies fall mainly under non-concessional IBRD loans.
